# Epic 2 Retrospective — A day that judges itself

## Epic summary

**Goal.** The author sets up his commitments, is asked each morning to answer for yesterday, and
watches days close and money accrue — the entire stakes mechanism running alone, before a second
person exists. **FRs covered:** FR-1, FR-2 (daily), FR-3, FR-9, FR-10, FR-13, FR-22, FR-25.

**Outcome, in one line: the mechanism is built and it runs by itself.** A day closes on a `pg_cron`
schedule, a penalty is written in the same transaction as the verdict, a notification leaves through
the outbox with nobody typing a command, silence closes a day against the author, and a chain
survives one bad day without wiping the others. The verdict below is nevertheless `rejected`, for
the same procedural reason Epic 1 was — see Acceptance verdict.

### Aggregate views

**A1 — `supersede_expiries()` writes a correction with no commitment outcomes, so a day the author
answered in time disappears from his chain.** *(correctness — the most consequential finding here)*

Source: `supabase/migrations/20260819241000_expiry_and_supersession.sql:175-236` is the **only**
definition of `public.supersede_expiries()` — `grep -rn supersede_expiries supabase/migrations/`
returns that file and nothing else. It inserts a correction row into `public.settlement` and, when
a slip was admitted, a `public.penalty`. It does not write `public.settlement_commitment`, which did
not exist when it was authored. The two later migrations that add outcome writes —
`20260819260000_chain.sql:254` and `20260819262000_summary_names_the_strongest_survivor.sql:95` —
both add them inside `settle_day` only.

`public.chain_current` (`20260819260000_chain.sql:74-113`) reads
`settlement_commitment sc join settlement_current s on s.id = sc.settlement_id`. After a
supersession the original settlement leaves `settlement_current`, taking its frozen outcomes with
it, and the correction brings none. The day contributes nothing to the chain at all — it is treated
as a day the commitment was not owed, which the view's own comment defines as "skipped, not broken".

Consequences, in the exact scenario `epics.md` Story 2.7 declares as an acceptance criterion
("I answered while offline and the submission was still queued when 48 hours elapsed … my answer
settles the day it belongs to, and any expiry already written is superseded"):

1. The day he answered — and held — is silently not counted. The chain reads one day short.
2. The Chains-detail calendar loses the day (see A2 for the surface that instead keeps the *wrong*
   version of it).
3. No summary is ever enqueued for that day. `settle_day` skips the summary for `expired`
   deliberately and `supersede_expiries` has no `outbox_enqueue` call, so the day that turns out to
   have been answered gets no evening sentence from either path.

The shipped SQL contradicts two written claims: `20260819260000_chain.sql:72` — "the chain follows
the correction rather than the original (AD-9)" — and
`spec-2-9-chains-that-survive-a-bad-day.md:158`, which tabulates *Superseded settlement → The chain
follows the correction*. The spec also records a live check that reports the opposite outcome
(`spec-2-9-chains-that-survive-a-bad-day.md:233`, "A correction moved the chain with it … took the
three chains to 3/3, 7/7 and 6/6"). Those chain numbers *grew*, which requires the correction to
have carried `held` outcome rows — something the shipped function cannot produce. The most likely
reading is that the verification built the correction by hand rather than by calling
`supersede_expiries()`. This retrospective has no database access and cannot settle which; either
way, the function as written does not do what the spec's decision table says it does.
*Disposition: fix now (code) — `supersede_expiries` must freeze the corrected day's outcomes the
same way `settle_day` does, and the live check must be re-run through the function itself.*

**A2 — the Chains detail reads a base table, against the epic's own stated rule.**
Source: `components/chains-detail.tsx:56` — `supabase.from('settlement_commitment')
.select('outcome,settlement:settlement_id(period)').eq('commitment_id', commitmentId)`. Every other
read in the codebase goes through a `*_current` view (`components/ledger.tsx`,
`components/today.tsx`, and this component's own chain read). Spec 2.7 claims "a check confirms no
component touches a base table" and `spec-2-9-chains-that-survive-a-bad-day.md:158` says the surface
"Reads `*_current`, never base tables". The check that exists — `lib/chain.test.ts:109`, "reads
through supersession rather than the base table" — asserts only that the *view's SQL text* contains
`public.settlement_current`. It does not look at any component, so the one place that breaks the
rule is outside its reach.

Combined with A1, the two surfaces disagree about the same day: the chain number drops a superseded
day entirely, while the calendar keeps showing the superseded verdict's `unanswered` marks forever.
*Disposition: fix now (code) — read the calendar through a view joined to `settlement_current`, and
widen the guard to the components.*

**A3 — a declared acceptance criterion was not built, not deferred, and not recorded.**
Source: `epics.md` Story 2.4 — "**Given** the morning hour that decides when I am asked / **Then**
it is configurable from Settings, alongside notification permission state and referee pairing, per
UX-DR17". `spec-2-4-answer-for-yesterday.md:134` carries the matching task:
`components/settings.tsx -- the morning hour, alongside notification permission state`. The file

public.profile to authenticated`) — so the back end is ready and there is no way in. Nothing in the
spec's Design Notes, its verification record, or `deferred-work.md`'s twelve entries mentions the
omission.
*Disposition: fix now — either build it or add a `deferred-work.md` entry; a silent gap is the one
outcome the spec discipline exists to prevent.*

**A4 — no task checkbox was ever ticked, in any spec, so the task list carries no completion

### Diff-scope review (narrowed)

`bmad-review` was not invoked and no sub-agents were used; the code lenses were run inline over a
narrowed scope — the migrations that cross story boundaries (2.5 settlement ↔ 2.7 expiry and
supersession ↔ 2.9 chains ↔ 2.8 summary) and the components that read them. That is where this
epic's risk concentrates, because no single session saw both sides. **A1 and A2 are the output of
that pass.** The TypeScript surfaces, the sign-in and commitment paths, and the whole of Stories 2.2
and 2.3 were not lens-reviewed here. An independent full-diff pass is likely to add findings.

One thing the pass looked for and did *not* find: the AD-5 / AD-9 interaction is correct.
`settlement_once` is split into a partial unique index on originals plus a unique index on
`supersedes`, so a correction is permitted and a chain cannot fork
(`20260819241000_expiry_and_supersession.sql:19-26`); `settle_day` infers the partial index in its
`on conflict … where supersedes is null` clause; `penalty_current` follows the chain so a superseded
penalty stops counting without losing its trace. The money path holds.

## Team discussion

Run after the findings above were written, with the installed room (Mary, John, Sally, Winston,
Amelia) reacting to them. Three things came out of it that the solo pass had not reached, and one
finding was substantially reframed. Every claim below is re-checked against its primary source.

**T1 — the deeper defect behind A1 is that none of the epic's verification records is
reproducible.** Counting lines in each spec's *"Verified end to end"* section that contain a runnable
statement (`select`, `insert`, `curl`, `psql`, `npx`, `supabase`): **spec 2.2 has one; the other nine
have zero.** `scripts/` holds `generate-icons.mjs` and `send-push.mjs` and nothing for verification.
So what the repository keeps is narrative, not evidence — and A1 could not be adjudicated in this
retrospective for exactly that reason. The room's fix is smaller than action item 4 was:
**commit the SQL that was already typed**, as `supabase/tests/<story>.sql`, runnable with `psql -f`.
Had that file existed for Story 2.7, tonight's A1 would have been a five-minute question.

**One correction to that conclusion, found while writing the first such file.** The room said "any
instance, including the live project with a throwaway account, no new infrastructure". That is not
quite true for anything touching settlement. `settle_day` **raises** rather than skipping when
`p_override` meets a profile with `is_live_doer` set (`20260819241000:105-110`), so one live account
disables the override path for the entire call, and the only way past it is to set
`app.settlement_invocation` by hand — precisely what AD-16 exists to prevent. Settlement tests
therefore need a database with no live doer: a local stack (`supabase start`, which needs Docker —
not currently installed here, and there is no `supabase/config.toml` yet) or a preview branch. The
room dissolved action item 4 slightly too fast: what it correctly killed was *writing a bespoke test
harness*, not *having somewhere to run one*.

**T2 — A3 was written smaller than it is.** `components/push-probe.tsx` describes itself as
"Produces a push subscription and shows it for copying into the send CLI" — it is Story 1.2's
diagnostic apparatus, and it remains the **only** route by which a user grants notification
permission (`components/push-probe.tsx:85` is the sole `Notification.requestPermission()` call in
the codebase). Meanwhile `morning_hour` is `not null default 7` with a `between 0 and 23` check and a
column grant (`20260819200000_declaration.sql:21-39`), and the gate returns as the entire screen
(`app/page.tsx`). So the finding is not "one component was not built": the product has **no Settings
surface at all**, its blocking gate is pinned at 07:00 with no way to move it, and the switch that
turns on its only delivery channel is a debugging tool. That is a story, not an action item.

**T3 — action item 4 dissolves into T1, and action item 6 splits.** The worker never inspects what
it sends — `supabase/functions/outbox-worker/index.ts:84` is
`webpush.sendNotification(..., JSON.stringify(row.payload))` — so moving sentence-rendering into it
is architecturally free, and would make `lib/summary.ts` and `payloadProblems` production code at
last. The obstacle nobody has tested: the worker is a Deno function importing `npm:` specifiers, and
nothing demonstrates it can import the Next app's `lib/` across the Supabase CLI bundler. Until that
is shown to *deploy*, "the worker renders" is an Epic 3 story with real risk, and doing it badly
creates a third copy of the rule rather than removing the second. What is cheap and can be done now:
relabel the three mirror modules as non-production, delete the false claim at `lib/expiry.ts:11`
("Mirrors `public.declaration_deadline`, and the tests hold them together" — they do not), and push
the payload rule down into a SQL `check` on `payload->>'body'`, where it would actually run.

**T4 — the `done` gate, set by `hwt75` during the discussion.** A story reaches `done` when the thing
it changed has a check that can be run again: **machine-checkable work needs a committed runnable
check; device-gated work is verified by the author on his own iPhone, and he moves it himself.**
That rule is what the Action items below are ordered by, and it makes the verdict's meaning change —
see Acceptance verdict.

Applying it to the current suite: no test anywhere calls `settle_day`, `supersede_expiries` or the
penalty write. `lib/ledger.test.ts` exercises `buildLedger`, the client-side fold that
`components/ledger.tsx` really uses; `lib/chain.test.ts:114` asserts a string is present in a
migration file. The stories whose executable coverage matches what actually runs are **2.2, 2.3, 2.4
and 2.6**. The stories where it does not are **2.1, 2.4a, 2.5, 2.7, 2.8 and 2.9** — and no component
has a test at all, so the UI half of 2.3, 2.6 and 2.9 is uncovered too.

## Acceptance verdict

**`rejected`** — criteria **declared** (per-story acceptance criteria in `epics.md` §Epic 2). Not
overridden: `hwt75` saw the finding and, rather than overriding, set the criterion (T4).

The machine verdict is forced by one fact — **all ten stories are at `review` and none is `done`.**
The first draft of this section then argued the result was procedural, and the team discussion
established that it is not. Under T4 a story is `done` when the thing it changed has a check that can
be run again, and by that standard the epic genuinely is not finished: **no test anywhere calls
`settle_day`, `supersede_expiries` or the penalty write.** The one function in the product that
decides money has no executable coverage of any kind. The `rejected` here is substantive, and the
technicality reading was wrong.

Two consequences follow, and both are more useful than the verdict itself. `review` stops being a
holding pen and becomes a queue where every entry names its own blocker. And the gate points
straight at the thinnest part of the epic: Stories 2.5 and 2.7 are the only two with no device
component, so nothing the author can verify by hand will ever sign them — they are blocked on a file
that does not exist yet.

On the declared criteria the substance is strong. FR-1 (create, edit, delete a commitment), FR-9
(the blocking morning Declaration, and 48-hour expiry), FR-10 (Day Close and Failed Day), FR-13 (a
flat 500,000₫ penalty written only by settlement, traceable, integer đồng), FR-3 (delivery through
the AD-3 outbox, proven unattended), FR-22 (the evening summary) and FR-25 (chains that survive a
bad day) are all built, and each is argued against its architecture decision in the migration that
implements it. The stakes mechanism runs alone, which is what the epic set out to prove.

Two things stand against acceptance on substance as well as on process:

- **A1 is a correctness defect on a declared acceptance criterion** — Story 2.7's offline-answer
  supersession case. It is not cosmetic: the author who answered honestly and on time loses a day of
  chain and gets no summary for it.
- **A3 is a declared acceptance criterion not delivered** — the Settings surface for the morning
  hour — with no record that it was dropped.

Neither is large. Both are the kind of thing that is cheap now and expensive once Epic 3 builds on
these tables. What converts this verdict is the Action items above in their order: the two
`supabase/tests/*.sql` checks first, because they are what makes any of the machine-only stories
signable at all.
