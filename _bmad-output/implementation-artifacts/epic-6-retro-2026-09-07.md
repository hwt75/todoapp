**F1 — CAP-6 is enforced at enqueue and nowhere else, and the gap is real, not theoretical.**
`enqueue_due_time_reminder()` refuses a window that has already opened
(`20260903090000_the_reminder_lands_inside_the_window.sql:322-334`), so nothing is ever *queued*
late. Delivery has no matching bound: `outbox_claim` selects on
`status = 'pending' and not_before <= now()` with no upper limit
(`20260819180000_outbox.sql:176-177`, read at source during this retrospective). A worker that is
down or backed up therefore delivers a due-time reminder whenever it recovers — after the window
has shut, which is the one thing CAP-6's `success:` line says must never happen. Nothing in the
suite or on a device has exercised the late-delivery case in either direction. **Disposition: fix
now** — either an expiry on the row or a delivery-time check, and a regression for it.

**F2 — CAP-6's other half cannot be closed by any evidence this repository can produce.**
The story says so itself (`spec-6-6-the-reminder-lands-inside-the-window.md:574-576`): nothing in
the suite waits for a minute to arrive, wakes the worker, or puts anything on a lock screen. The
five device checks at `:578-587` remain unrun, story `6-6` remains `review`, and
`epic-6-retro-item-41` is the only `open` action item in `sprint-status.yaml`. **Disposition:
carry forward unchanged** — this is the item that keeps the epic unaccepted.

### Divergences from the spec

**D5 — the SPEC's three Open Questions about objections were answered inside Story 6.7 and never

### Adversarial, edge-case and verification-gap lenses

Run as three parallel sub-agents over the epic's product code, weighted at the seams between
stories. Every finding below was re-checked against its source before being routed; the ones that
did not survive that check are not here.

**A1 — an `evidence` row with no stored object holds a day. HIGH, open.**
`commitments_owing()` turns a timed claim into `held` on the bare existence of a metadata row
(`20260829090000_midnight_decides_the_day.sql:326-328`), and the only shape check on that row is
`storage_path like (parent_id || '/%')`
(`20260903120000_a_photo_i_can_keep_against_any_commitment.sql:100-102`) — a string pattern, not a
reference to `storage.objects`. Nothing on the insert path verifies an object exists. Verified at
source during this retrospective. **Failure:** one `POST /rest/v1/evidence` with a fabricated
`storage_path` and no upload at all settles the day `clean`, avoiding the penalty and keeping the
chain. The suite certifies the behaviour rather than catching it: `tests/6-4:193,247,542` each
insert exactly such a bare row and assert `held`. **Disposition: fix now.**

**A2 — the referee reads claim photos the app calls private. HIGH, narrowed today, still open.**
`lib/evidence.ts:281`, rendered at `components/today.tsx:705`, says *"It is private — only you can
open it."* Story 6.8 narrowed the referee's evidence policy by parent kind only (`commitment_id is
null`), deliberately leaving declaration-parented photos visible to him. Today's per-account scoping
narrowed the blast radius from every account to his own doer, but for that doer the sentence is
still false. **Disposition: fix now — one of the two has to change, the copy or the policy.**

**A3 — a window may legally end at 24:00, leaving no time to attach the photo the day is judged
on.** `20260828130000:55-70` accepts `23:30 + 30`; a claim at 23:58 lands, and the evidence insert
is refused the instant midnight passes (`20260903120000:144-149`), so the day fails for a photo the
product made unattachable. **Disposition: defer, with the case recorded.**

**A4 — a claim queued offline and flushed after midnight can never be proven.** The queue copy says
it will go "dated when you tapped"; the claim then lands for yesterday and every photo for it is
refused. Neither surface says so. **Disposition: defer.**

**A5 — a claim that lands with an unread id leaves no upload control until the app is reopened.**
`lib/declaration-write.ts:99-107` fetches the declaration id in a second round trip; on failure the
row renders as claimed with no `Proof` input, and the read effect depends only on `[localDay]`
(`components/today.tsx:358`). A flaky connection at 20:05 costs the day's photo.
**Disposition: defer.**

**A6 — `apply_grace_days()` is the last correction writer that rebuilds from live state.**
`20260825110000:436-438`, where `object_to_day()` and `rule_appeal()` were both moved onto frozen
rows this month. **Disposition: fix now** — it is the same defect class twice repaired.

**A7 — verification gaps.** No test asserts a refusal for an evidence row whose object is absent
(A1's blind spot); none claims inside a window ending at 24:00 and then tries to prove it; none
states what a referee may read of a *declaration*-parented photo (A2). **Disposition: fix now,
alongside A1 and A2.**

## Behavior verification

Unusually thorough for this project, and it is where the two most valuable findings came from.
Recorded honestly, including what was not exercised.

**Exercised against the live product, signed in as a real account** (the maintainer signed in; this
skill does not enter passwords):

- **Creating the first commitment on a fresh account blocked the whole app** behind *"Did TEST —
  proof photo hold on 2026-09-06?"* — a day before the commitment existed, which
  `commitments_owing()` returns nothing for. Found by clicking the first button a new user clicks.
  Fixed the same day (`20260907150000`, commit `77978b5`); the client mirror had never carried the
  `created_at` clause the server has had since `20260824090000`.
- **The evidence path end to end**: upload → object in `appeal-evidence` under
  `<commitmentId>/<uuid>`, `evidence` row with `captured_on = for_day`, thumbnail rendered from a
  signed URL at its true 64×64. It works.
- **The kept photo has no textual signal.** After a reload the only evidence a photo exists is a
  bare thumbnail; `"Proof saved."` is transient, and the sole text is the alt attribute
  (*"Photo 1 of 1 you kept."*), which a sighted reader never sees. A screen-reader user is told;
  everyone else has to notice an image. **Disposition: fix now, small.**
- **The referee invitation refusal** the maintainer reported was the `is_live_doer` guard working
  exactly as written, not a defect — and the product had no re-pairing path at all.

**Exercised against the database, on the live project:**

- Two referees, each paired to their own doer, after the per-account change. A real RLS session
  probe showed the original referee reading **18** settlements — his own doer's — where the whole
  project holds **89**. Before today he could read all 89, including 31 belonging to
  `dnthang300@gmail.com` and 20 each to two more accounts he has no relationship with. That leak
  was live for the whole of Epic 4 through 6.

**Not exercised:** Story 6.6's five real-device checks — a locked phone, a real minute arriving, a
push landing. Nothing in this repository can substitute for them, and the story says so itself.

## Acceptance verdict

**rejected.**

Criteria were **declared**, not profiled: `SPEC.md` carries CAP-1 through CAP-8, each with an
explicit `success:` line, plus the approved 2026-09-03 scope change.

The machine verdict is forced by `pending_stories`, which holds exactly one key:
**`6-6-the-reminder-lands-inside-the-window`**. Under the rubric an unfinished story makes the
verdict `rejected` regardless of everything else, and the maintainer was shown that list and chose
to retro over it. This is not a judgement about the quality of the work — it is that the epic is not
finished.

Independently of that, CAP-6 is the capability that is not met: its `success:` line says a reminder
must land inside the window and *never* after it has shut, and that is enforced at enqueue only.
`outbox_claim` has no upper bound (`20260819180000:176-177`), so a backed-up worker delivers
whenever it recovers. Seven of the eight capabilities are met on the evidence; CAP-6 is met in the
half this repository can prove and unproven in the half it cannot.

## Open questions

- **Does a timed day nobody claimed need to say so?** It settles `expired`, mints a penalty and
  sends no summary (`20260829090000:479-482,494-497,511`), deliberately and with a stated reason —
  but the SPEC's own Success signal says the forgotten evening "fails on its own, **visibly**".
  Whether Story 5.2's silence intervention covers that is a product judgement, not a code one.
- **What should CAP-6's delivery-side bound be** — an expiry on the outbox row, or a check at send
  time? Either changes what a late worker does with a queued reminder.
- **Two accounts carry `is_live_doer`.** Which one is the real doer, and should the other be
  cleared?
- **Story 6.9 and the orphan sweeper are both scope no approved artifact added.** The work is sound;
  the proposal and the SPEC should be reconciled to reality either way.
