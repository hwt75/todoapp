## Findings

### Aggregate views

**F1 — Story 1.3 was built without a spec, against the project's own convention.**
Source: `_bmad-output/implementation-artifacts/` holds `spec-1-1-installable-shell.md` and
`spec-1-2-push-arrives-on-locked-phone.md`, but no `spec-1-3-*`; `story-1-3-findings.md` exists at 115
lines. The work is well documented after the fact, so this cost nothing here — a research spike whose
deliverable is a written finding is the case where a spec adds least. But the convention was departed
from silently, and nothing records that a spike may skip it.
*Disposition: fix now (process).*

**F2 — No commit subject carries a story key, so per-story attribution is impossible.**
Source: `git_evidence.py --range 40b37ba..cf3421b` returned `"stories": []` for **all 10 commits**. The
retrospective tooling could not attribute a single commit to a story; the attribution in this document
was reconstructed by hand from subjects and file paths. Subjects say "Story 1.2" while the
sprint-status key is `1-2-a-push-arrives-on-a-locked-phone-and-survives-a-reboot`, and the matcher
requires the key.
*Disposition: fix now (process).*

**F3 — The `frozen-after-approval` block of spec 1.2 was rewritten, with no recorded renegotiation.**
Source: `spec-1-2-push-arrives-on-locked-phone.md` was created in `2b1d9c1` and modified in `f1021c3`;
diffing the `<frozen-after-approval>` block between those commits shows the Intent substantively
replaced. The original Approach was to build the production path — Supabase schema, transactional
outbox, an Edge Function worker signing Web Push, and a `pg_cron` schedule. The replacement proves the
channel with a local CLI and defers all of it.

The tag reads `reason="human-owned intent — do not modify unless human renegotiates"`. The *decision*
was sound and is argued well in `deferred-work.md` — fusing an assumption test with infrastructure
would have left a finished outbox serving a product that had just lost its delivery channel — and the
outcome vindicates it. What is missing is any artifact recording that the human renegotiated. The
control exists so intent cannot drift silently; here it drifted correctly, but silently.
*Disposition: fix now (process) — record the renegotiation retroactively, or re-freeze with approval.*

**F4 — As-built diverges from AD-3, by design and on the record.**
Source: `ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md:79` — "AD-3 — Everything outbound goes through the transactional
outbox"; `scripts/send-push.mjs` sends Web Push directly from an operator CLI. This is a sanctioned
deviation: argued in the spec's Design Notes and carried in `deferred-work.md` with the work to close
it. Recorded here so it stays visible as a live divergence rather than becoming precedent.
*Disposition: defer (tracked in `deferred-work.md`).*

**F5 — No god classes, no duplication growth.**
Source: `wc -l` across all source files — 825 lines total, largest `components/push-probe.tsx` at 120,
`scripts/send-push.mjs` at 119, everything else under 100. Device- and browser-dependent rules were
extracted into pure functions (`lib/install-state.ts`, `lib/push-payload.ts`) with 31 tests, which is
the epic's own stated constraint.
*Disposition: accept — this is a win.*

**F6 — Churn is dominated by `package-lock.json` (+9328/−547).**
Source: `git_evidence.py` per-file churn. Normal for a project's first dependency graph; noted so a
future reader does not mistake this for a large epic. The three icon PNGs carry `binary_revisions: 1`
each — unmeasured churn, not zero churn.
*Disposition: accept.*

### Diff-scope review

The code lenses were run over this exact range immediately before this retrospective, at high effort,
rather than re-run here. Three findings, all confirmed by execution rather than inspection, all in the
operator-facing error paths of `scripts/send-push.mjs`, and all fixed in `cf3421b`:

1. A truncated paste of the subscription file was reported as a *missing* file, instructing a full
   reinstall — expensive because iOS offers the notification permission prompt once. That paste was
   not hypothetical; it happened while running the story.
2. A malformed VAPID key produced a raw Node stack trace as the primary message, which the spec's own
   I/O matrix rules out.
3. `npm run push` could not run without a `.env`, so the script's carefully written guidance was
   unreachable through the documented command.

Source: `cf3421b`, and the five failure paths re-exercised after the fix.
*Disposition: fixed.*

**F7 — Process lesson: a conclusion was published from a single observation, then corrected.**
Source: `e5aef4e` recorded a push as lost and derived an architectural constraint from it — that `201`
could not be trusted as delivery, and that the deferred outbox would need device-level
acknowledgement. `3aa2799` corrected it one commit later: the push had arrived, late, because the
phone had not yet rejoined Wi-Fi after its reboot.

The recovery was handled well — the correction is written into the findings with the reasoning
preserved rather than swapped in silently, and the surviving constraint is much smaller. The lesson
stands anyway: on a channel with known store-and-forward latency, "I checked and it wasn't there" is
not the observation "it never came", and an entire piece of infrastructure hung on the difference.
*Disposition: accept (lesson recorded).*

## Behavior verification

Runtime behavior was exercised end to end, on the target device, not inferred from tests.

- **Four real pushes to the author's own iPhone**, all `201` from `web.push.apple.com`, all delivered:
  immediate on a locked phone; after a reboot; through an offline window (queued and delivered on
  reconnect); and after 3h 42m with the app untouched, against a one-hour requirement. Bodies were
  legible on the lock screen without unlocking. Source: `story-1-2-findings.md` rows 1, 2a, 2b, 3.
- **The production build was exercised in a real browser** before deployment: service worker
  registered and reached `activated` with scope `/`, `/sw.js` served as JavaScript carrying both the
  `push` and `notificationclick` handlers, manifest served `display: standalone` with 180/192/512
  icons, no console errors.
- **Story 1.3's negative result was established by running requests**, not by reading documentation:
  every path on `tryhackme.com` — API, profile page, `robots.txt` — returned `429` with
  `X-Vercel-Mitigated: challenge`, reproduced from two independent network egresses. Source:
  `story-1-3-findings.md`.

## Acceptance verdict

**`rejected`** — machine verdict, criteria **declared** (per-story acceptance criteria in `epics.md`
§Epic 1). Rendered with no human decision; a human may override.

The verdict is forced by a single fact: **all three stories are at `review` and none is `done`**, and
an unfinished story forces `rejected` regardless of substance.

That mechanical result should not be read as the epic having failed. On the declared criteria the
evidence is strong:

- *"a push is sent from a server, and the phone is locked and rebooted to see whether the notification
  survives"* — done, four times, on the author's own device, with the idle test run to nearly four
  times its required duration.
- *"TryHackMe completion history is checked for external readability"* — done, and settled negative
  with reproduced evidence; FR-8 reduced to the timer Auto-check alone, exactly as the criterion
  provided for.
- *"the only epic whose valid outcome may be stop"* — it did not stop, and the reason it did not is
  documented rather than assumed.

What is genuinely incomplete is Story 1.1's token layer, deliberately withheld so a negative push
result would cost no design work. That insurance has now expired unused, and the work is unblocked but
unbuilt. Closing it, plus review sign-off on 1.2 and 1.3, is what converts this verdict.
